# 864 — Tool Belt (DS1)

Dark Souls III-style tool belt as a DS1 tweak mod. Issue stays
`actionable`: slice 1 is lab-verified, the native slice is designed with
grounded leads but unproven.

## Requirement

Backport Dark Souls III's Tool Belt concept: a dedicated belt separate
from the normal quick-item cycle, holding eligible utility and frequently
used items, with the quick-item bar and cycling preserved, belt access
distinct from the pouch tweak (#861) so both coexist, and controller and
keyboard equivalents.

  eligibility is the game's own rule (`isEquip == 1`, 95 goods

  (doubled names carry their id); "Belt N" mark text.

## Static findings for slice 2

- Quick items are rawSlots 15-19 (loop at 0x495D00, `cmp ebx, 5`); full
  map 0-3 weapons, 4-7 ammo, 8-11 armor, 12-13 rings, 14 reserved.
- Selections at equip+0x8C/+0x90 (hands), +0x94/+0x98 (arrows),
  +0x9C/+0xA0 (bolts). The use path reads each slot twice: live at
  equip+0x24 and saved at assembly+0x24, plus the actor assembly at
  player+0x840. The island must write all three and restore them.
- Equip-change shape at 0x7468CB (live write + notify 0x31B540);
  weapon/ammo use bound `cmp ebx, 7` at 0x749B91; quantity translators
  0x747860/0x747920 carry no quick codes.
- HUD: `F20-00_item` (icon + count) under `F20-00` with the
  `text_item_name` popup; frame hook 0x39761A shared with every input
  tweak. Pad commits at +0x86/+0x87/+0x92 are unidentified.
- DS3's belt (separate equipment slots for multiplayer/utility items,
  start-menu access, 10 quick slots left free) confirmed from player
  docs; no belt/pouch names exist in any of the three exes, and none of
  DS2 Scholar's 45 menu classes hints at a belt, so the backport
  reimplements the concept natively. See the mod `sources.md`.
- Unmapped: selected-quick field, Use-Item (X) and d-pad pad bytes,
  quick-use consumer, keyboard routing, belt-strip placement,
  multi-frame use timing. Two dataflow scans came back empty: no
  selection access through an inline-derived equip register (779 unique
  game-data sites), and no actor-assembly (+0x840) selection access
  through an inline-derived world chain (2174 refs). The switch path
  takes its pointers as arguments; map it live.
